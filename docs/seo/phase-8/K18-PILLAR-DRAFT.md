## 3. Body copy (approx. 1,500 words)

### What K18 means

K18 is gold that is 18 parts in 24 — **75% gold** — with the remaining 25% an alloy of metals such as silver, copper and palladium that sets the hardness and the colour [S7][S10]. It is the standard purity for fine jewelry in Japan [S10]. Our Jewelry Guide's **Metals and stamps** section explains this in full, shows where the stamp sits on a ring, a clasp or an earring post, and lists every stamp our catalogue carries (K24 down to K10, the platinum grades, silver 925) with what each means [S10]. Read that first if you are new to stamps; this guide starts where it stops.

### K18, 18K, 750, Au750: four spellings, one purity

The same 75% purity is written four ways depending on where a piece was made and marked. **K18** (letter first) is the Japanese convention; **18K** (number first) is used in English-speaking markets; **750** is the purity in thousandths, the convention in Europe and the form the Japan Mint uses; **Au750** adds the chemical symbol [S10][S5]. They denote the same gold content [S10].

One practical note from the Japanese resale trade: because "18K" is the convention of overseas and older pieces, some buyers in Japan treat an 18K stamp with extra care — a minority of such pieces have been found to assay below 75% [S5]. The stamp records what the maker marked; it is not itself a test. That is why a stamp is read together with a weight check and, where present, the Mint mark (below), and why Cha Jewels hallmark-checks every preloved and branded piece in Japan before listing it [S10]. We show each stamp exactly as it is struck on the piece [S10].

### K18 hallmarks used in Japan: the maker's stamp and the Mint mark

Two different marks can sit side by side on a K18 piece.

**The maker's purity stamp** — K18 or 750, usually with a maker's mark beside it — is struck by the manufacturer and states the gold content [S10]. The Japan Mint's own page is explicit that marks such as "K18" and "Pt900" are **not** the Mint's certification marks [S1]; they are the maker's statement.

**The Japan Mint hallmark** (造幣局の品位証明記号, called ホールマーク) is a certification struck by the Japan Mint after it has tested the piece. It has three elements: the **Japanese flag**, which identifies the Japan Mint as the issuer; a **diamond-shaped frame** containing the purity in parts per thousand; and, on platinum, a **platinum symbol** [S1]. For gold the Mint's grades are **999 (K24), 916 (K22), 750 (K18), 585 (K14), 416 (K10) and 375 (K9)** [S2]; platinum has four grades (900 is the example the Mint gives) and silver five (925 is the example) [S2]. A K18 piece that carries the Mint mark therefore shows a flag beside a diamond reading 750.

Three things follow. First, the system is **voluntary**: the Mint tests and marks "at the request of businesses that manufacture or sell precious-metal products", so a genuine K18 piece may carry no Mint mark at all [S1]; retailers note that custom-made pieces in particular usually lack it [S3]. Second, a "750" struck on its own, without the flag and frame, is a maker's purity stamp, not a Mint certification [S1][S8]. Third, the Mint's numbering changed for pieces accepted from April 2012 — pure gold became 999 instead of 1000, and a few grades were renumbered or discontinued — so an older piece can carry a mark that the current table does not list and still be certified [S2].

Where one of our pieces carries the Mint mark, the listing says so. The **N1616 Necklace K18 16.65g M-Cut 16 Sided Triple Kihei 0.7mm 40cm** is one: its description states that a Mint Bureau assay mark is engraved [S9a]. Most of our K18 pieces carry the maker's K18 stamp alone, which is normal [S1][S3].

### K18 vs K24 (and K14, K10) in daily wear

K24 is 99.9% gold or purer [S7]. Pure gold is soft; K24 jewelry scratches and deforms more easily, which is why K18 — harder because of its alloy — is the metal most often used for rings and necklaces worn every day [S5][S7]. The alloy also gives K18 its colour range: yellow, pink and white gold are all K18 with different alloy mixes [S5][S10]. Going the other way, K14 (about 58% gold) and K10 (about 42%) contain more alloy again and are harder still, but hold less gold per gram [S10]. For chains in particular — Kihei above all — K18 is the usual choice, and K24 chains are bought with the understanding that they will mark [S4][S5]; our Kihei guide covers that choice in detail.

### How gold jewelry is weighed, and why the gram figure is on every listing

Every Cha Jewels gold listing carries its gram weight in the product name — **B4618 Bracelet K18 4.60g Oval Snake Chain 18cm**, **N8550 Necklace K18 11.95g Oval Snake Chain 50cm**, **8C100 Necklace K18 100g 8 Cut Kihei 50cm** [S9d][S9e][S9b]. The weight is the whole piece as sold, clasp included, read on a jewellery scale; where a listing says "approx." it is because a fresh weighing can differ by a few hundredths of a gram, and because stones and findings are included in the figure [S9a].

Why it matters: for plain gold pieces — chains, bangles, plain bands — Japanese shops quote and buy by gram weight as the base, then adjust for cut, design, brand and condition [S5]. The gram figure is therefore the single most comparable number between two listings of the same purity, which is why we never list a gold piece without it. For pieces with stones, the gram weight includes the stones, so the stone's own weight (carats) is listed separately — the **R7813 Ring K18/PT900 7.80g Diamond 0.172ct** shows both [S9c]. Weight is not purity: a heavy K10 piece contains less gold than a lighter K18 piece [S10]. Read the stamp and the weight together.

(This guide does not state any per-gram price or rate: our prices are set per piece and shown live on each product page.)

### K18 jewelry authentication in Japan

What we do before a preloved or branded piece is listed, and what you can do with a piece you own:

1. **Find and read the stamp** — on the inside of a ring band, the flat tab of a necklace clasp, or an earring post [S10]. Expect K18 or 750 for gold; Pt850/Pt900/Pt950 for platinum [S10].
2. **Look for the Mint hallmark** — the flag and diamond [S1]. Present: a certified purity. Absent: not a fault, because the system is voluntary [S1][S3].
3. **Weigh the piece** and compare with the stated weight; a gold listing with no weight is a listing to question [S5].
4. **Check that the metal colour and wear are consistent** with K18 — the alloy makes K18 harder than K24, so a piece that dents like pure gold or shows a different-coloured base metal at worn edges is not what its stamp says.
5. **Match the paperwork** — for diamonds and gemstones, a certificate where one exists (our FAQ explains when a certificate is included); for branded pieces, the brand's own markings.
6. **Ask.** Our FAQ explains what "preloved" means, how authenticity is handled and which services (polishing, resizing, repair, certification) we offer.

Cha Jewels' own commitment, from the Jewelry Guide: "Our preloved and branded pieces are hallmark checked in Japan before we list them." [S10]

### Buying K18 jewelry in Japan: what to read on a listing

A good K18 listing in Japan tells you, before you ask: the **stamp** (K18/750 and whether a Mint mark is present), the **gram weight**, the **length or ring size** (Japanese ring sizes are in 号; the Jewelry Guide's ring-size section converts them), the **clasp type**, the **condition** (new, or preloved with its wear described), any **stone weights and certificates**, and — for branded pieces — the brand as marked on the piece. Our product names carry stamp, weight, size and style in that order so the grid can be scanned, and the product page carries the rest. The **Shopping Guide** covers ordering, payment, shipping and returns; the **Wholesale** page covers trade purchases (minimum 10 pieces, 5 business days, JPY or PHP, as stated there). Customers in Japan and the Philippines can **reserve with layaway** on the English site; the down payment and monthly amounts are quoted live by our system on each product page, never in this guide.

### Care

K18 is harder than pure gold but still a soft metal by everyday standards. Wipe pieces after wear, store them apart so faces and settings are not scratched, keep them out of hot-spring water (sulphur), shampoo and seawater, and wash only with lukewarm water and a little neutral detergent when needed [S4][S5]. The Jewelry Guide's **care** section is the full version, including platinum and pearls; this guide defers to it.

## 5. FAQ block (for FAQ JSON-LD; render visibly, then mark up)

1. **What does K18 mean?** — Gold that is 18 parts in 24, i.e. 75% gold, alloyed with metals such as silver, copper and palladium for hardness and colour [S7][S10].
2. **Are K18, 18K, 750 and Au750 the same?** — They denote the same 75% purity written in different conventions; K18 is the Japanese form, 750 the thousandths form the Japan Mint uses [S10][S2].
3. **What is the Japan Mint hallmark?** — A voluntary certification mark: the Japanese flag beside a diamond-shaped frame containing the purity in thousandths (750 for K18), applied by the Mint at a maker's or seller's request after testing [S1][S2].
4. **Is a K18 piece without the Mint mark genuine?** — It can be. The Mint system is voluntary, so many genuine pieces carry only the maker's K18 or 750 stamp; the stamp is read together with a weight check [S1][S3][S5].
5. **Why is the gram weight in every product name?** — For plain gold pieces, weight is the base of how the trade in Japan values them, and it is the most comparable number between two listings of the same purity [S5].
6. **Is K18 or K24 better for jewelry worn daily?** — K18: it is harder, so it resists scratches and deformation better than K24, which is purer but softer [S5][S7].
7. **How does Cha Jewels check preloved K18 pieces?** — Every preloved and branded piece is hallmark checked in Japan before listing; the stamp is shown as struck and the weight is stated [S10].
